## Hero

**We own the model.**

Aperia runs its own large language model — an open-weights model, fine-tuned on Aperia's proprietary payments knowledge and hosted entirely inside our own infrastructure. It is not a rented API. It is a proprietary asset we have built, trained, and control.

The conversational-analytics agents our merchants and ISOs use every day are powered by this model. The customer experience is Aperia's, the intelligence behind it is Aperia's, and the data never leaves our boundary.

## The asset

Aperia operates a self-hosted large language model built on **Llama 3.1**, an open-weights model licensed for commercial use, deployment, and modification. We run it on our own infrastructure and have fine-tuned it on Aperia's proprietary domain knowledge — the merchant, ISO, risk, and payments expertise accumulated across the platform.

Two things make this an owned asset rather than a vendor relationship:

- **The model runs inside our boundary.** The weights execute on Aperia-controlled infrastructure. No customer data is sent to a third-party AI provider, and there is no per-call dependency on an outside vendor's pricing, availability, or terms.
- **The intelligence is ours.** The fine-tuned model — the version that actually answers our merchants' and ISOs' questions — is a derivative we created from our own data. That fine-tune, the training data behind it, and the deployment around it are wholly Aperia's intellectual property.

---

## Why an open model we own — not a closed API we rent

A closed model (a typical hosted AI API) is a black box we could only ever rent. The provider holds the model; we send data out and get answers back; we pay per call for as long as we use it; and we never possess the thing doing the work. The capability, the economics, and the roadmap all sit with the vendor.

An open-weights model is different. We hold the weights. We run, modify, fine-tune, and deploy on our own schedule, inside our own security boundary. That distinction is what lets an LLM become an asset on Aperia's books rather than a line item on a vendor's invoice — and it is the foundation of everything below.

---

## Aperia Knowledge, embedded

The base model is a commodity available to anyone. Our advantage is what we have put inside it.

We have embedded Aperia's proprietary knowledge — payments domain expertise, merchant and ISO context, risk and compliance logic — directly into the model through fine-tuning. This is the moat. A competitor can license the same open base model tomorrow; what they cannot replicate is a model trained on Aperia's data and tuned to Aperia's business.

Because we own the pipeline, this compounds. Every improvement to our data and every additional fine-tuning cycle deepens a proprietary capability that only Aperia possesses, and that gets more valuable the longer we run it.

## What this delivers

**Owned IP.** The production model is a proprietary Aperia asset — fine-tuned on our data, running on our infrastructure, ours to version, improve, and control. Not a service we rent.

**A widening moat.** The base model is open to all; the Aperia-knowledge fine-tune is ours alone and compounds with every training cycle.

**Data control & compliance.** Everything runs inside Aperia's boundary. Customer data never leaves for a third-party model — a fit for a PCI-regulated payments platform, and a differentiator we can stand behind with enterprise clients.

**Predictable economics.** Self-hosting replaces an open-ended per-token meter with fixed, controllable infrastructure cost that scales with our own capacity planning, not a vendor's price list.

**Independence.** No vendor lock-in, no exposure to a third party's pricing changes, deprecations, or availability. The capability is ours to run on our own terms.
